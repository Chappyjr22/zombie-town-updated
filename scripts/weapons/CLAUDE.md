# scripts/weapons

Owns: weapon base class, per-weapon stats/behavior (fire rate, damage, reload, hitscan/projectile), muzzle flash and impact effects.

Depends on: `assets/models/weapons` (see `ASSET_MANIFEST.md` for which models exist), `resources/weapons/*.tres` (per-weapon data).

## Status

- `weapon_data.gd` — a `Resource` (`class_name WeaponData`) holding one weapon's stats + asset references (model scene, fire/reload sounds, damage, fire rate, mag size, reload time, range). No behavior, pure data.
- `weapon_controller.gd` — a `Node3D` (`class_name WeaponController`) that equips a `WeaponData`, hangs it off the character's hand, and owns the fire/reload logic: cooldown from `fire_rate`, semi-auto vs automatic via `is_automatic`, two-stage hitscan (see below) that calls `take_damage(amount)` on anything hit that has that method, and a reload timer that refills from reserve ammo. It emits `ammo_changed`, `fired`, `reload_started`, and `aim_changed`; the player uses `fired`/`reload_started` for upper-body-only third-person animations layered over locomotion. The hitscan excludes the shooter's own physics body (`owner.get_rid()` — `owner` is the `Player` scene root since `WeaponController` is a direct in-scene child of `player.tscn`, not a separately instanced sub-scene) so aiming close to your own feet can't self-hit.

**The Insta-Kill power-up** (`scripts/economy/power_up_drop.gd`) overrides `_effective_damage()` outright (`999999.0`) rather than multiplying it, checked via `owner.has_instakill()` - `owner` is the `Player`, same reference `_hitscan()` already excludes from its own raycasts. A player-wide timed buff, not a per-weapon stat, so it's read from the player rather than duplicated as a `WeaponController` var. **Max Ammo** is `refill_all_ammo()` - loops `loadout` and calls the existing `refill_weapon()` per slot, rather than being a separate refill implementation.

**A dead zombie used to keep blocking shots aimed at whatever stood behind it.** `die()` disabled the movement capsule but never touched `HitboxArea` (a separate, wider `Area3D` - see `scripts/ai/CLAUDE.md`), which stayed on `PhysicsLayers.HITBOX` and kept getting found by raycasts for the corpse's full `corpse_lifetime`. Fixed on the `zombie.gd` side (`hitbox_area.collision_layer = 0` in `die()`), not here.

**`WeaponData.penetration_count`** lets one pellet hit more than one zombie in a straight line, continuing past whoever it just hit rather than stopping. 0 (every current weapon) is unchanged single-target behavior. `_fire_pellet()`'s ray is now built in segments: the first reaches only to `extended_aim_point` (just past whatever the crosshair is actually on - unchanged, and correct for a single target), but a penetrating continuation reaches the *full* `weapon_range` instead, since the 0.15m crosshair overshoot was never meant to clear a second target standing further back. Each segment gets its own tracer/impact and damage/splash/chain resolution; world geometry (or anything without `take_damage`) always stops the chain outright, penetration budget or not.

### There is no viewmodel

The game is **third person**, so there is exactly one weapon in the world. `attach_world_model()` hangs it off the body's `mixamorig_RightHand`, and that single instance is what the player and everyone else sees.

Two parts of the fit are measured rather than authored, so a new weapon usually needs no tuning:

- `_fit_to_grip()` scales the model so its longest dimension is `weapon_length`, turns it barrel-down-`-Z` via `_canonical_weapon_basis()` (which infers the barrel/up/width axes from the bounding box, since the pack's FBXs are authored every which way), and slides it so `grip_anchor` lands where the hand closes.
- `_align_world_model()` then turns it to lie along the line between the body's *own two hands*, sampled from the animated pose one frame after attaching. That's why the weapon points where the animation holds it in any clip, instead of at an angle guessed against Mixamo's arbitrary wrist frame.

**One-handed weapons take a different line.** With `uses_support_hand = false` (the two handguns), there is no second hand on the weapon to measure against, so `_align_world_model()` extends the *forearm* through the wrist instead — which is how a pistol is actually pointed — and `_solve_support_arm_ik()` is skipped entirely so the off arm swings the way the pistol clips authored it. Measured across the whole loadout by `tools/probe_weapon_stances.gd`, both rules land the barrel within 0.98 of the view direction.

Recoil is a spring on the **camera** (`_update_recoil`), not on a weapon model.

**A connecting shot emits `hit_confirmed(part, killed)`.** The player turns it into points and the HUD turns it into a hitmarker; the controller deliberately knows about neither. `part` comes from the target's own `classify_hit()` (see `scripts/ai/CLAUDE.md`) and is asked for **before** the damage lands — a zombie that dies to the shot stops animating, and the head bone the classification measures against moves when it does, so asking afterwards would score the corpse's pose rather than the shot.

**Hitscan is two-stage** (`_hitscan`). The camera sits metres behind the player, so a single ray straight down it would start behind the character and hit whatever stood between them. Instead a first ray from the camera finds what's under the crosshair, then the damaging trace runs from the weapon to that point. Both rays set `collide_with_areas = true`, because a zombie's actual hittable volume is a separate, wider `Area3D` on `PhysicsLayers.HITBOX` (see `scripts/ai/CLAUDE.md`) rather than its (deliberately tight) movement capsule - if a ray hits that area instead of the zombie's `CharacterBody3D` directly, `_hitscan` resolves `target` to `result.collider.get_parent()` before checking for `take_damage`/`classify_hit`.

**The second ray overshoots `aim_point` by 0.15m along its own direction, rather than stopping exactly on it.** `aim_point` is a surface intersection found from the *camera's* ray; the second ray starts at the *muzzle*, a different point, and approaches that same coordinate from a different angle. A ray that terminates precisely on a target's surface can numerically graze past the volume instead of crossing into it - found by testing: `tools/probe_round_loop.gd`'s shooting check went from reliable to a coin-flip once the player's default loadout and the hitbox setup (above) changed the exact numbers involved, at the close range (5m) that check uses. Fixed by extending past the point instead of trying to make the two rays agree on geometry they were never guaranteed to.

The game was first person for a while, with a conventional viewmodel — arms carved out of the soldier, weapon posed in camera space, hands pinned and IK'd onto it. It's gone. `docs/ASSET_PIPELINE.md` records why, so the same ground isn't covered twice.
- `resources/weapons/*.tres` — one `WeaponData` instance per weapon model in `assets/models/weapons/`, mapped to the closest-matching fire/reload sound in `assets/audio/weapons/` (see that file's header comment or `ASSET_MANIFEST.md` for the mapping).
- Wired into `scenes/player/player.tscn` as `CameraRig/SpringArm3D/Camera3D/WeaponController`, carrying all eight weapons, starting on the AK-47.

## Mystery box wonder weapons

Five weapons only obtainable from `scripts/economy/mystery_box.gd`, never wall-buyable - `resources/weapons/ray_gun.tres`, `ray_gun_mk2.tres`, `thundergun.tres`, `wunderwaffe.tres`, `war_machine.tres`. Stats (damage, mag size, splash/chain numbers, cost) are ported directly from `zombie-town-online`'s own weapon table (`chappyjr22/zombie-town-online`, `public/index.html`'s `def({id:'raygun',...})` blocks) - that source data was already balanced against the exact same round/health curves this project ported wholesale for the base economy (see `scripts/ai/CLAUDE.md`'s round table), so it's used as-is rather than re-derived. Two mechanics didn't exist before these needed them:

- **`WeaponData.splash_damage`/`splash_radius`** (Ray Gun, Ray Gun Mark II, War Machine): `_apply_splash_damage()` damages every zombie within `splash_radius` of the impact point besides the one directly hit, tapering linearly from `splash_damage` at the impact point to 0 at the radius. Independent of the direct hit's own `damage` - the source data gives each its own number, not a fraction of the other (War Machine is 0 direct damage, 420 splash: every kill is the splash, never the primary hit).
- **`WeaponData.chain_count`/`chain_radius`** (Wunderwaffe DG-2): `_apply_chain_damage()` hops from the struck target to the nearest not-yet-hit zombie within `chain_radius`, up to `chain_count` times, each hop dealing the same damage as the initial hit (the source data has no separate falloff term for chained hits).
- **`WeaponData.cone_kill_range`/`cone_kill_angle_degrees`/`cone_kill_damage`** (Thundergun): `_hitscan()` detects this (`cone_kill_range > 0`) and calls `_fire_cone()` instead of tracing any ray at all - every zombie within range and half-angle of the muzzle dies outright, full stop. The original dealt 0 direct damage and killed through a knockback impulse that flung zombies off the map; this project's ragdoll never got past round 7 of tuning (see `scripts/ai/CLAUDE.md`) and `die()` currently plays a canned animation instead of simulating one, so there's no impulse system to sell the original effect with - a flat, enormous damage number gets the same actual gameplay promise (anything this close in front of you is dead) without pretending to have physics it doesn't. **Aimed with the camera's forward vector, not `get_barrel_direction()`** - unlike every other weapon here, this one is gameplay-affecting on its aim direction (it decides who dies), and testing found the two-handed world-model alignment `get_barrel_direction()` depends on can come out unreliable for several frames after equipping (support-hand IK/alignment settling). Every other use of barrel direction in this file is cosmetic (spine aim, ADS) and already tolerant of that; this one couldn't be.

**None of the five has its own model yet.** Each currently reuses an existing wall-buy weapon's mesh (`ray_gun`→`makarov.tscn`, `ray_gun_mk2`→`luger.tscn`, `thundergun`→`ak47.tscn`, `wunderwaffe`→`sniper.tscn`, `war_machine`→`grease-gun.tscn` - picked to spread them across different silhouettes, not for any thematic reason) with `WeaponData.tint_color` set to a distinct color per weapon. `attach_world_model()` calls `_apply_weapon_tint()`, which - only when `tint_color.a > 0` - walks every `MeshInstance3D` under the model and sets `material_overlay` to an unshaded, additive-blended flat color, so the original texture/shading stays visible under a colored glow rather than being replaced by it. This is a placeholder, not a real reskin: new models are ChatGPT's side of the project (see `docs/ASSET_PIPELINE.md`), not this session's - swap `model_scene` on each `.tres` once real ones exist and delete `tint_color`'s value (alpha 0) to stop overlaying it.

`thundergun.tres` originally reused `shotgun.tscn`. Switched to `ak47.tscn` after testing found `get_barrel_direction()` coming out wildly wrong (pointing down and to the side, changing every time alignment re-ran) specifically for that model - `ak47.tscn` didn't show the same instability, though the cone-kill fix above (aiming off the camera instead) means this no longer actually matters for Thundergun's own accuracy. Worth understanding *why* the shotgun fit was unstable before trusting `get_barrel_direction()` for anything gameplay-critical again.

`scripts/economy/mystery_box.gd` (`class_name MysteryBox`) grants a random entry from a `wonder_weapons: Array[WeaponData]` export, preferring one the player doesn't already own (falls back to the full pool once every entry is owned, rather than refusing to grant anything once someone's collected the set) - matches the source's own flavor text ("it will not hand you something you are already carrying"). Cost defaults to 950 if unset ("nine hundred and fifty a roll"). Placed once in `tools/build_town_level.gd`'s `_place_economy()`, opposite the fountain from Pack-a-Punch - **not verified against the level visually**, this session has no way to look at `town.tscn` in-editor; check it isn't clipping anything.

**Simplified from the source data**: Ray Gun Mark II was a 3-round burst (`burst:3,burstRate:700`) - this project has no burst-fire mode (`WeaponData.is_automatic` is just semi/full-auto), so it's semi-auto instead, same damage/mag/reload. Worth adding real burst-fire support later if it's worth the difference.

### Flare Gun - a real weapon, not a wonder weapon

`resources/weapons/flare_gun.tres` uses `scenes/weapons/flare-gun.tscn`, which already existed in the project - fully socket-tuned (`Grip`/`Foregrip`/`Muzzle` markers already placed) and modeled from `assets/models/weapons/cc0/flare-gun.glb`, part of the same CC0 pack as the current 8 wall-buy weapons (see `ASSET_MANIFEST.md`) - but never wired to a `WeaponData` or placed in a level until now. Uses the same `splash_damage`/`splash_radius` fields as the wonder weapons above (40 direct + 190 splash, both well below the wonder-weapon numbers - a wall-buy weapon, not a box exclusive, so it's priced and balanced against the existing roster instead of ported from the source's own flare gun stats, which were tuned for a different economy). Wall-buy price 1400, placed 2m along the same wall as the pistol ammo buy in `_place_economy()` - also not verified visually.

## Known gaps / simplifications

- **Shotguns fire a cone of pellets.** `WeaponData.pellet_count`/`pellet_spread_degrees` drive `_hitscan()`, which now loops that many independently-jittered rays (`_fire_pellet`/`_random_cone_direction`) instead of always firing one. `damage` is still the total for the shot - split evenly across pellets in `_hitscan()` - so Pack-a-Punch and Double Tap keep multiplying one number regardless of how many rays it's spread across, and every non-shotgun weapon is unaffected (`pellet_count` defaults to 1, `pellet_spread_degrees` to 0, which collapses back to the original single straight ray). `shotgun.tres` is 8 pellets in a 3.5-degree half-angle cone, `damage` unchanged at 70 - point-blank, the cone is tight enough that all 8 still connect for the old one-shot number; at range, pellets increasingly miss, which is the falloff a real shotgun should have and the single-ray version couldn't produce. A target already killed by an earlier pellet in the same trigger pull is skipped rather than re-scored, so multi-pellet kills don't stack duplicate kill-bonus points.
- **Weapon switching** is wired: `loadout` on the `WeaponController` holds the carried weapons, number keys 1-9 pick a slot and the scroll wheel cycles. Ammo is stored per slot in `_stored_ammo`, so switching away and back is not a free reload, and switching is refused mid-reload so it cannot be used to cancel one and keep the rounds. **The model swap itself defers to a holster clip when the outgoing set has one.** `equip_slot(slot)` asks `owner.play_holster_animation(current_weapon.locomotion_set)` *before* touching anything - the player plays the one-shot (if that set's `"holster"` key resolves to a real clip - `assets/animations/rifle/holster.res` exists, no other set has one yet) and returns its length; `equip_slot()` stashes the requested slot in `_pending_slot`/`_switch_timer` and returns without swapping. `_process()` counts that timer down and performs the actual swap (`_perform_equip_slot()`, the old `equip_slot()` body) once it hits zero - no firing, reloading, or further switching happens while `_pending_slot != -1`. A set with no holster clip gets `0.0` back and swaps instantly, exactly as before this existed. `grant_weapon()`'s "replace the weapon at cap" branch (a wall-buy purchase) deliberately bypasses this and stays instant - a purchase-triggered swap reads differently from a combat weapon-cycle, and routing it through the same slot-index-unchanged path as `equip_slot()` doesn't cleanly apply anyway (the slot doesn't change, only its contents do).
  **Bug found by testing: a second switch requested mid-holster used to be silently refused, not queued.** The raw Mixamo draw/holster clips run ~2 seconds (not fast), and `equip_slot()` bailed outright whenever `_pending_slot != -1` - the first key pressed won, and every switch requested before that first one finished vanished with zero feedback. With `test_arena.tscn` handing the player every weapon specifically to cycle through quickly, this read as the game locking up rather than as dropped input. Fixed: a *different* target now replaces the pending one instead of being refused (`current_weapon` hasn't actually changed yet mid-holster, so this is just re-requesting the same holster clip against a new destination, restarting the one-shot rather than stacking a second one behind it), and requesting the slot still actually in hand cancels the pending switch back to it. Mashing through several weapons now always settles on whichever was requested last, never on whichever happened to be pressed first.
  The draw side is unchanged by this: `scripts/player/player.gd`'s `_on_weapon_changed()` still plays a one-shot from `LocomotionSets.SETS[set]["draw"]` once the swap (whether instant or holster-delayed) actually lands. Both `rifle/draw.res` and `pistol/draw.res` exist now.
  **A weapon can't be fired while sprinting.** `_process()`'s `wants_fire` check reads `owner.get("is_sprinting")` (`Player.is_sprinting`, set every physics frame) - ordinary FPS convention, and specifically requested alongside the pistol set finally getting a real sprint clip (`pistol/sprint.res`, reused across all eight blend-square directions since only one capture exists - see `locomotion_sets.gd`'s own comment on that set).
- **Weapons are seated by three `Marker3D` sockets** on their `scenes/weapons/*.tscn`: `Grip` and `Foregrip` are wrist positions, `Muzzle` sits on the bore and its `-Z` facing is the barrel direction. Placed by hand in the editor against the model, which is the only reliable way — the hand sockets are offset from the weapon by the thickness of a hand, so nothing about the hold can be inferred from the silhouette. `_fit_to_grip()` falls back to the old bounding-box heuristic only for weapons without sockets.
- **`WeaponData` carries two body-side fields**, both read by `scripts/player`: `locomotion_set` picks which animation pack the character carries the weapon with (`rifle` or `pistol` — see `scripts/player/locomotion_sets.gd`), and `uses_support_hand` decides whether the off hand is pulled onto the foregrip.
- **The player starts with one weapon** (`player.tscn`'s `loadout` is `[pistol, null, null, null, null, null, null, null]`) and fills the rest via `scripts/economy/wall_buy_station.gd`. `grant_weapon()` fills an empty slot up to `weapon_cap` (2, raised to 3 by Mule Kick) or replaces the currently-held weapon once at cap — matching "buying a wall weapon swaps your current gun" rather than a drop prompt.
- **Pack-a-Punch and Speed Cola never mutate `WeaponData`.** `load()` caches and shares one `Resource` instance across every reference to a given `.tres`, so writing to `current_weapon.damage` directly would upgrade that weapon for every slot/player holding it, not just the one that got packed. Instead: `_pack_level: Dictionary` (int tier per slot, same per-slot pattern as `_stored_ammo`) and `reload_speed_multiplier` (a plain var, set by `scripts/economy/perk_machine.gd`'s Speed Cola) are read at the point of use — `_effective_damage()`/`_effective_mag_size()`/`_effective_reserve_cap()` — rather than baked into the resource.
- **The support hand's rotation is corrected**, eye-tuned in the editor. `_solve_support_arm_ik()` puts the wrist on `foregrip_anchor`, then maps the hand's local +Y (Mixamo runs it down the fingers) onto `get_barrel_direction()` in skeleton space, rather than aligning the hand's whole basis to the weapon's — that was tried first and pointed the fingers straight up over the barrel instead of around it. `support_hand_rotation_degrees` is the remaining tunable offset for closing the gap between "roughly wraps the foregrip" and actually sitting on it — shipped as an untuned `(-90, 0, 0)` starting guess at first, since eyeballing it needs a running game to look at; tuned live via the Remote scene tree's inspector once the game was actually playable end to end, landing on `(-15, -60, 5)`. A probe confirms the math produces a valid, finite orientation for every support-hand weapon, and settles rather than drifting (see below).
  **Built fresh from `barrel_local` every frame, not as a delta nudged onto the hand's own current basis** - an earlier version of this read the hand's current finger direction and corrected *from* there, which compounds: next frame's "current" already includes this frame's `support_hand_rotation_degrees` offset, so the offset doesn't apply once, it reapplies on top of itself every frame, forever. Caught by a probe sampling the finger direction over 40 frames and watching the per-frame delta - the buggy version wouldn't have been diagnosable by looking at any single frame, only by watching it over time. The two-bone elbow/shoulder solve above was never susceptible to this in the first place: `desired_elbow - current_elbow` measures the actual current angle fresh each frame rather than assuming last frame's write stuck, which is what makes it self-correcting instead of cumulative - the hand-rotation fix now follows the same shape.
- **ADS narrows the FOV and aligns the sight - by two entirely different mechanisms depending on perspective.** `scripts/player/player.gd`'s `_update_camera_distance()` shrinks `CameraRig`'s own over-the-shoulder X offset toward `camera_aim_shoulder_offset` (0.15, down from the hip-fire ~0.45) on the same lerp that already pulls `spring_length` in, in both perspectives. What happens to the *weapon* diverges from there.
  **Third person nudges the camera to chase the weapon's sight** (`_update_ads_sight_alignment()`, unchanged from before first person existed): `SpringArm3D`'s position (not the camera itself - see that function's comment for why) gets nudged so the crosshair lines up with `get_sight_position()` (`sight_in_mesh` in world space - see `WeaponData.sight_nudge` for per-weapon correction), clamped to `ADS_SIGHT_CLAMP_THIRD_PERSON` (0.6m) and eased in by `aim_blend`/`ads_transition_speed`. This works because the *weapon* isn't itself derived from the camera in third person - correcting the camera doesn't move the target it's chasing. **Third person is untouched by everything below** - both `_apply_first_person_weapon_hold()`/`_solve_grip_arm_ik()` gate on `owner.get("is_first_person")` and no-op entirely otherwise.
  **First person pins the weapon to the camera instead of chasing it, in both hip-fire and ADS** (`_apply_first_person_weapon_hold()`, renamed from `_apply_ads_weapon_hold()` once it stopped being ADS-only). A nudge-based correction was tried first for ADS, reusing the third-person mechanism with a much bigger clamp, and **could not converge**: found by probe to plateau at a stable but real ~0.12-0.2m residual no matter how generous the clamp, because in first person the *camera* is what the weapon's position was derived from (indirectly, through the animated grip-hand bone and `Player._apply_spine_aim()`'s torso twist trying to point it at the camera) - nudging the camera just moved the thing the correction was chasing by the same amount, every frame, forever. Worse, since the weapon was still bone-driven, the "aligned" moment lasted exactly as long as the idle animation held still, which was never actually still - and that same mismatch turned out to still be there in **hip-fire** too, just never scrutinized the way ADS was: `Player._update_first_person_camera()` tracks the head bone rigidly regardless of aim state, so a bone-driven hip-fire weapon was drifting relative to a camera that never moved with it, the whole rest of first-person play.
  `_apply_first_person_weapon_hold()` sidesteps the fight entirely rather than winning it: `world_model.global_transform` is set directly to `camera.global_transform * (a local hold transform)` throughout first person, hip-fire included, completely replacing the bone-driven pose instead of correcting it - nothing here depends on the skeleton's current pose at all, so there's nothing left for an idle clip to disagree with. **Two different holds, blended by `aim_blend`.** ADS's own position is solved *backwards* from `ads_sight_target` (a fixed point in camera-local space, `(0, 0, -0.35)` by default) so that `sight_in_mesh` lands exactly there once transformed - `ads_hold_rotation_degrees` is a free, purely cosmetic rotation. Hip-fire has no target to solve for (nothing needs to land on the crosshair while not aiming), so `hip_fire_hold_position` is a plain fixed offset instead, with `hip_fire_bob_amount`'s figure-8 sway (`owner.velocity`'s horizontal speed drives a sine bob, vertical at double frequency - dips every step) added on top. `world_model.global_transform = hip_hold.interpolate_with(ads_hold, aim_blend)` - at `aim_blend = 1` this uses `ads_hold` exclusively, so the bob fades out on its own while aiming with no separate "stop bobbing" logic needed. No blend *into* first person from the bone-driven pose when `is_first_person` toggles on - the weapon snaps into the held pose the same frame as the camera's own accepted pop on toggle, rather than adding transition machinery for an infrequent, deliberate user action.
  **The hip-fire hold's position is constrained by real arm length, unlike the ADS one** - a first guess (`(0.15, -0.22, -0.4)`) put the target 0.735m from the grip-arm's shoulder, well beyond the ~0.478m the arm's own two bones can actually reach (measured by probe on the AK-47), leaving the hand visibly short of the gun by ~0.23m. Not the kind of thing that shows up unless something is specifically measuring hand-to-grip distance - caught by probe, not by eye. Current default (`(-0.05, -0.30, -0.05)`) keeps the target within reach with margin; if this is retuned, sanity-check the new distance from the shoulder stays comfortably under ~0.45m before spending time on how the pose looks, or the arm physically can't reach what's being tuned.
  **Both holds' rotation must be built from `_world_model_canonical_basis`, never anything bone-derived - a real bug, caught by screenshot** (the camera ended up looking at the side of the receiver instead of down the sights, on an otherwise-perfect-reading sight alignment). `sight_in_mesh`/`grip_in_mesh`/`foregrip_in_mesh`/`barrel_in_mesh` are all raw *mesh-local* points - "authored every which way" per `_canonical_weapon_basis()`'s own comment, the barrel could be any axis - and only mean anything once mapped through a rotation that puts the barrel down -Z. `world_model.transform.basis` (post-`_align_world_model()`) looks like that rotation but isn't purely that: `_align_world_model()` *post-multiplies* a second rotation onto it that re-aims the weapon along the line between the character's current hands, entangling the pure mesh-to-canonical fit with wherever the grip-hand bone's animated pose happened to be at the one moment alignment ran. Using it for the hold reproduced that same hand-pose dependency in camera space, with nonsense results (barrel 110-170° off the camera's own forward, measured by probe) - and it wasn't caught by the sight-alignment residual, because the position solve absorbs *whatever* rotation it's given and still lands `sight_in_mesh` exactly on target regardless of whether the rest of the gun is oriented sanely. `_world_model_canonical_basis` is the fix: `world_model.transform.basis` cached in `attach_world_model()` immediately after `_fit_to_grip()` sets it, *before* `_align_world_model()` (which only runs the following frame, off `world_model_aligned`) ever touches it - pure mesh-to-canonical, no hand pose baked in. Re-verify with a probe checking `get_barrel_direction()` against the camera's own forward if this is touched again, not just the sight-alignment residual - the residual alone can't tell a correctly-oriented gun from a backwards one.
  This makes ADS's crosshair alignment **exact by construction and immune to any animation running underneath it** - confirmed by probe (both position and orientation): sight residual settles under 0.001 and barrel-to-camera-forward angle settles under 0.5°, and both *stay there* across 180+ frames of continued idle animation, instead of plateauing at 0.12-0.2m like the nudge approach did. Hip-fire is held to the same standard - confirmed stable relative to the camera (not absolute world position, which still moves with the camera's own head-bone idle sway by design) once settled, with `hip_fire_bob_amount`'s effect measured matching its own formula almost exactly. `_update_ads_sight_alignment()` is skipped outright in first person now (its own correction would be circular here, chasing a target that moves with it) - not just superseded, actively wrong to leave running.
  **The grip hand needs its own IK once decoupled from the bone it's normally attached to - now throughout first person, not just ADS.** `_solve_grip_arm_ik()` mirrors `_solve_support_arm_ik()`'s two-bone solve (both now call a shared `_solve_two_bone_arm()` helper) but targets `grip_in_mesh` (same shape as `foregrip_in_mesh`, captured by both fit functions) instead of the foregrip, and uses `GRIP_UPPER_ARM_BONE`/`GRIP_FOREARM_BONE`/`GRIP_HAND_BONE`. Without this the grip hand would keep following its own animated pose while the gun it's supposedly holding sits locked in place a few centimetres away - it was never needed before because the weapon being bone-attached made hand and weapon coincident by construction. No hand-rotation wrap the way the support hand gets (`support_hand_rotation_degrees`) - the grip hand's own animated grip pose reads fine once its position is corrected; only the support hand needed a rebuilt-from-scratch rotation to stop draping over the barrel.
  **`sight_nudge` is re-applied every frame** (`_recompute_sight_in_mesh()`, called from `_process()` before `_apply_first_person_weapon_hold()`/`_update_ads_sight_alignment()`, both of which read `sight_in_mesh`), not baked in once at equip time the way it otherwise would be. Split into `_sight_fit_base` (the fit-time point before the nudge) and `_sight_fit_transform` (identity for the socket-marker path, `out_of_fit` for the bounding-box fallback - the two fit functions apply the nudge in different spaces, see `_fit_to_grip`'s own comment) so the nudge itself can be recombined cheaply every frame instead of re-running the whole fit. Exists specifically so dragging `Sight Nudge` on the currently-held weapon's resource in the Remote inspector (`WeaponController → Loadout → [slot] → Weapon Fitting → Sight Nudge`) shows up immediately.
  **The default `sight_in_mesh` heuristic itself was biased too low, and only for the socket-marker fit path.** The original `_sight_fit_base = grip.position + along_barrel * 0.5 + Vector3.UP * 0.04` landed within 4cm of grip height on every weapon - fine as a rough starting guess in third person's forgiving margin, but nowhere close to a real top-mounted rail/sight (found via probe on the M4A1: heuristic point at `Y=-0.061` against the model's own bounds running all the way to `Y=+0.088`). `_fit_to_grip()`'s bounding-box fallback path already biased its own version of this to the exact top of its bounds ("top-centre of the bounds is where iron sights and rail optics sit on every model in the pack" per that path's own comment) - `_fit_from_sockets()` just never got the same treatment. Fixed by measuring the model's raw-mesh-space bounds (`raw_bounds`, captured *before* `model.transform` is reassigned to the fitted frame, so it's in the same space `grip.position`/`along_barrel` already are) and using its exact top Y instead of the flat `+0.04`. Surveyed by probe across the full 14-weapon loadout after the fix: every single weapon now lands with `sight_nudge = (0,0,0)` at exactly the top of its own bounds - no per-weapon tuning needed at all for most guns now, `sight_nudge` is a correction for the exceptions, not the whole job. (One exception found and fixed: the M4A1's old manually-tuned `sight_nudge = (0, 0.13, 0)`, dialed in against the *old* too-low default, was now double-correcting and overshooting 49% past the top of the model - reset to `(0,0,0)` in `assault_rifle_2.tres`.)
  **A live debug visualization exists for exactly this kind of tuning** (`debug_show_sight_marker`, `WeaponController` export, toggle live in the Remote inspector). Draws two small spheres, updated every frame: green at `get_sight_position()` (where the code currently thinks the sight is), orange at `camera.global_transform * ads_sight_target` (where first-person ADS is trying to put it) - they should sit exactly on top of each other once `aim_blend` reaches 1 in first person. Built lazily, freed (not just hidden) when toggled back off. Exists because live-tuning `sight_nudge`/`ads_sight_target`/hold values with no way to see what they're actually doing to the geometry - just re-launching and squinting at the render - was the actual source of hours of tuning fatigue, not the values themselves being hard to find. Worth reusing for the attachment system's optic sight-point once that gets built (see this project's own planning notes).
  **Verify with a probe, not by eye, if this is touched again**: an artificially high `Engine.time_scale` in a headless probe (tried 50x) destabilizes `move_and_slide()`/gravity badly enough to drag the whole character toward the floor, which then reads as a huge, spurious alignment error that has nothing to do with the alignment code. 6x (matching every other probe this session) is stable; that's what turned up the crouch conflict below, not a code bug.
  **`CameraRig.position` has two independent owners already** - `_update_crouch()` in player.gd rewrites `.y` every physics tick unconditionally in third person (skipped outright in first, see `scripts/player/CLAUDE.md`) and `_update_camera_distance()` rewrites `.x`/`.z`. Third person's ADS sight nudge deliberately lands on `SpringArm3D` instead, one level down, specifically to stay out of that. Any future system touching this rig needs to pick yet another node, or coordinate with the existing owners instead of writing to `CameraRig.position` directly.
- **The weapon doesn't point at the crosshair while sprinting.** `player.gd`'s spine yaw correction converges to zero error at idle and while jogging, but is deliberately released during a sprint: Mixamo's sprint clip swings both arms, so closing the gap would need ~70° of torso twist and the character would run hunched. You aren't aiming mid-sprint, and shots trace from the camera regardless.
  The correction reads the barrel via `get_barrel_direction()`, which derives it from `barrel_in_mesh` — measured at fit time. **Do not** take the model's local `-Z`; `_align_world_model()` post-multiplies a rotation, so that axis stops being the barrel. Getting this wrong is what made the first attempt diverge.
- **Muzzle flash, tracers, and impact sparks are procedural - no texture or model assets.** `_spawn_muzzle_flash()` (once per trigger pull, from `_try_fire()`), `_spawn_tracer()`, and `_spawn_impact()` (both once per pellet, from `_fire_pellet()` - a shotgun blast fans out into several short streaks rather than reading as one line) build everything from primitives: an `OmniLight3D` + billboarded `Sprite3D` for the flash, a thin stretched `BoxMesh` for a tracer, a small one-shot `GPUParticles3D` burst for an impact. All three share one texture, `_get_glow_texture()` - a soft white circle generated once at runtime via `GradientTexture2D`, not shipped as an asset. This is deliberate: new texture/model art is ChatGPT's side of the project (see `docs/ASSET_PIPELINE.md`), not this session's, so the VFX here are built only from things GDScript can generate or Godot ships built in. Every spawned node is parented under the tree root, not the weapon or the scene - the weapon is bone-attached and would drag a flash along with the next frame's animation, and `current_scene` isn't guaranteed to be set in every context (e.g. a bare probe scene) - and frees itself off its own tree via a one-shot `SceneTreeTimer`, so it outlives a reload or weapon switch instead of being cut short by one. A miss still draws its tracer, out to the ray's `extended_aim_point`; only a hit spawns an impact.
  Colors/intensities/sizes (`recoil_stiffness`-style tuning, not exposed as `@export`s yet) are a first pass, not eyeballed against the actual models in-editor - worth a look once there's a build to run.
- `take_damage(amount)` is called on raycast hits and is implemented by `scripts/ai/zombie.gd`.
